## 1. IBD / William O'Neil: 197 industry groups, Group RS Rating, the "top 40" rule, the "half the move" claim and independent CAN SLIM tests

### Takeaway
IBD ranks 197 industry groups by **relative price performance over roughly the past six months**. The exact formula is proprietary. MarketSmith describes it as a least-squares curve fit on summed member prices, with separate weightings for different periods. Stocks get an Industry Group RS letter grade (A+ to E), and the practitioner rule is to buy from the top 20 to 40 groups. The supporting evidence is almost entirely O'Neil's own research [VENDOR]. Independent tests cover CAN SLIM as a whole, never the group rank in isolation.

### Cited Findings
- **Number of groups and lookback:** IBD splits listed companies into 197 groups, and the rankings list "the top-performing groups over the past six months in terms of relative price performance" — [Yahoo Finance / IBD: Industry Group Rankings Help You Pick Top Stocks](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/industry-group-rankings-help-pick-204300919.html) [VENDOR]
- **Calculation (closest to official):** a group rank is "a proprietary number obtained by calculating the least-squares curve fit on summed prices on certain stocks within that industry". A further calculation is then run over all companies in the group, with "separate weightings ... for different time periods" — [MarketSmith India: Industry Groups](https://marketsmithindia.com/mstool/industrygrouplist.jsp) [VENDOR, partly disclosed]
- **The stock's Industry Group Relative Price Strength Rating:** it compares the price performance of the stock's industry over the last six months with the other 196 industries. It is separate from the stock's own 12-month RS Rating (1–99) — [Yahoo/IBD](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/industry-group-rankings-help-pick-204300919.html); for the stock RS of 1–99 over the trailing 52 weeks versus the whole database, with leaders "typically" above 80 when their big runs begin: [Nasdaq: Stocks with rising relative strength](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/stocks-rising-relative-strength-waters-2017-10-20) [VENDOR]
- **Letter scale:** the Industry Group RS Rating runs from A+ to E. No breakdown of the cut-offs for each letter is published — [Capforce: Making Sense of IBD Composite Rankings](https://www.capforceetf.com/insights/leadership-in-one-number-making-sense-of-ibd-composite-rankings) [secondary]
- **A group's RS Rating is "the primary gauge"** on which IBD's industry rankings are built (an article about 13 years old) — [Yahoo: Faltering Relative Strength Is A Subtle, Valuable Sell Signal](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/faltering-relative-strength-subtle-valuable-212300871.html) [VENDOR]
- **Top-20/40 rule:** IBD advises looking at the top 20 industries "or maybe the top 40" and staying away from the bottom 20 groups — [Yahoo/IBD](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/industry-group-rankings-help-pick-204300919.html); winners are found "in the top 40 of 197 IBD industry groups" — [iTiger repost of IBD: Find Leading Stocks In Leading Industries](https://www.itiger.com/news/2501746788) [VENDOR]
- **Unofficial reconstruction of the IBD RS formula (stock level, often applied to groups too):** weights 0.4 / 0.2 / 0.2 / 0.2 on the price ratio over roughly 63/126/189/252 days (most recent quarter weighted double) — [TradingView RS Rating script](https://my.tradingview.com/script/lsLzfQqs-RS-Rating-Multi-Timeframe); [Elite Trader thread](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/where-can-i-find-industry-group-ranking-online-similar-to-ibds.31233/); open-source implementation: [GitHub skyte/relative-strength](https://github.com/skyte/relative-strength) [UNOFFICIAL]
- **"Half of the move" claim:** O'Neil states in *How to Make Money in Stocks* that about **37%** of a stock's price movement is tied directly to its industry group and another **12%** to its sector, so roughly half comes from group/sector — [Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/how-to-make-money-in-stocks-pdf-william-j-oneil); [Trading Momentum Substack](https://tradingmomentum.substack.com/p/why-the-markets-biggest-winning-stocks) [VENDOR; the underlying study is not published, and the figures were seen only through secondary summaries]
- **AAII CAN SLIM screens (whole system, includes the group component):** AAII states the screen is its own "interpretation" and was not set by O'Neil. It runs 3 variants (2nd edition, revised 3rd edition, no float). Its hypothetical results: the original screen averaged **24.4%/year** from late 1997, with a risk index of 1.89 (89% more risk than the S&P 500); the revised screen returned 743.8% cumulative, **23.8%/year**, through the end of 2007 — [AAII: O'Neil's CAN SLIM Revised 3rd Edition](https://www.aaii.com/stockideas/article/10668-oneils-can-slim-revised-3rd-edition-approach); [AAII: How to Use the CAN SLIM Approach](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/how-to-use-the-can-slim-approach-to-screen-for-growth-stocks); [AAII screen 76](https://www.aaii.com/stocks/screens/76); [AAII: Tribute to William O'Neil](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/68036-a-tribute-to-william-o-neil-revisiting-the-can-slim-strategy) [THIRD PARTY but hypothetical, unaudited, no transaction costs]
- **Schadler & Cotton (2008)**, using AAII screen data for 1998–2005, report an annualised **30.86%** for the CAN SLIM portfolio (as cited by Lutey) — [Lutey, OPBM II, Journal of Accounting and Finance](http://www.na-businesspress.com/JAF/LuteyM_LWeb14_5_.pdf) [INDEP, but on AAII data]
- **Replication criticism:** AAII does not disclose portfolio constituents or statistical analysis, and the CAN SLIM criteria are hard to automate, which makes academic replication difficult — [Applied Finance Letters 2023: Towards a Simplified CAN SLIM Model](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372201250_TOWARDS_A_SIMPLIFIED_CAN_SLIM_MODEL) [INDEP]
- **Statistical test of AAII's 54 screens** (whether screens beat the market) — [ResearchGate: Are the AAII stock screens a useful tool for investors?](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253779303_Are_the_AAII_stock_screens_a_useful_tool_for_investors) [INDEP; the snippet did not state the CAN SLIM result specifically]
- **Thomas Bulkowski** has a page titled "Industry Relative Strength", apparently testing chart-pattern performance by industry RS — [ThePatternSite: Bulkowski on Industry Relative Strength](https://thepatternsite.com/industryRS.html) [INDEP practitioner; the page could not be fetched, so no results are recorded]

### Inferences
- The essential feature of IBD's group rank is a **price-based (not fundamental), six-month, recency-weighted, cross-sectional percentile** across about 200 groups. The "top 40 of 197" rule works out to roughly the top 20%.
- The "37% + 12%" claim is consistent in spirit with academic findings that industry is a major part of stock co-movement and momentum (see Moskowitz & Grinblatt, section 3). But the O'Neil figure itself has no published methodology, so treat it as a vendor claim.
- No independent test isolates the IBD group-rank component's contribution to returns. CAN SLIM tests mix the group component with EPS, new highs and other filters.

### Gaps
- The official cut-offs for the A+ to E letter grades (for example, whether A = top 20% of groups) were not found. investors.com could not be fetched.
- The exact weights and periods in MarketSmith's "least-squares curve fit" are not disclosed.
- Bulkowski's industry RS results were not obtained (the domain was blocked).
- A primary citation (book page/edition) for the 37%/12% figure was not verified; only secondary summaries were seen.

## 2. Zacks Industry Rank: average Zacks Rank (estimate revisions), ~265 industries, the "top 50% beat bottom 50% by 2 to 1" claim, and independent evidence

### Takeaway
The Zacks Industry Rank is the **average Zacks Rank (1–5, driven by earnings estimate revisions) of an industry's member stocks**, recalculated daily and ranked across about 265 "expanded" industries. The top half (#1–132) is considered favourable. The "2 to 1" claim comes from Zacks' own 10-year backtest (2008–2017, weekly rebalance) [VENDOR]. No independent verification of the Industry Rank itself was found. Academic evidence on industry-level revisions or earnings news gives only indirect support.

### Cited Findings
- **Construction:** 265 industries; each industry's rank is the average Zacks Rank of its member stocks, computed daily. The top 132 = top 50%, the bottom 133 = bottom 50% — [Zacks: Zacks Industry Rank](https://www.zacks.com/zrank/zacks-industry-rank.php); [Zacks industry page example](https://www.zacks.com/stocks/industry-rank/industry/Computer-OpticalImaging-138) [VENDOR]
- An older Zacks version says "approximately 250 industry groups", with the average Zacks Rank computed for each industry every trading day — [Zacks commentary](https://www.zacks.com/commentary/1884162/keep-your-portfolio-in-the-green-with-this-strong-buy-stock) [VENDOR; the industry count has changed over time]
- **"2 to 1" claim:** the 265 industries were split into a top half (best average Zacks Rank) and a bottom half, and each half was compared against the S&P 500 over **2008–2017** with a **one-week rebalance**: "the top half beat the bottom half by a factor of more than 2 to 1" — [Zacks: how to identify stocks with the best profit potential](https://www.zacks.com/commentary/2054749/how-to-identify-stocks-with-the-best-profit-potential); [Zacks Industry Rank page](https://www.zacks.com/zrank/zacks-industry-rank.php) [VENDOR; the return measure (cumulative/annual, equal/cap weighting), costs and dispersion were not disclosed in the extracts]
- **Horizon:** the Zacks Rank is a short-term rating, "most effective over the one- to three-month holding horizon" — [Zacks commentary](https://www.zacks.com/commentary/1884162/keep-your-portfolio-in-the-green-with-this-strong-buy-stock) [VENDOR]
- **Indirect academic evidence (industry-level information):**
  - Hui, Nelson & Yeung (2013): there is a large post-forecast-revision drift tied to **industry-wide** earnings news but none tied to firm-specific news. They attribute it to investors underreacting to the greater persistence of industry-wide earnings — [Journal of Accounting Research 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1475-679X.12006); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v51y2013i4p701-737.html) [INDEP]
  - Howe, Unlu & Yan (2009): changes in **aggregate** analyst recommendations (I/B/E/S, 1994–2006) predict future market excess returns, and analysts' information sets contain market- and industry-level information — [Lehigh PDF: The Predictive Content of Aggregate Analyst Recommendations](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/AggRecJAR.pdf) [INDEP]
  - Analyst forecast revisions are predictable and form an implementable strategy (firm level) — [SSRN: Jung, Keeley, Ronen](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2991938) [INDEP]

### Inferences
- Zacks' industry signal is **fundamental (estimate revisions)**, not price-based, so it complements IBD/RS-style systems. Its natural horizon is short (1–3 months), which matches the weekly rebalance used in the "2 to 1" test.
- "Beat by a factor of 2 to 1" probably refers to the ratio of the two halves' cumulative or average returns, not a long-short spread. Without the full methodology it cannot be translated into alpha.
- The Hui et al. (2013) finding that drift is concentrated in the industry-wide component is the strongest academic support for using aggregated revisions at the industry level.

### Gaps
- No independent study (SSRN/AAII) testing the Zacks Industry Rank specifically was found.
- The return figures (in %) behind the "2 to 1" claim, and any results after 2017, were not obtained (zacks.com could not be fetched).
- The relationship between the "Expanded Industry" (265) and "Sector" (16) levels and any sector-level test was not verified.

## 3. MSCI Barra industry momentum factor (USE4 / GEM / GEMTR), and the academic basis (Moskowitz & Grinblatt)

### Takeaway
MSCI documents an explicit **Industry Momentum** factor in the **Barra Global Equity Trading Model (GEMTR)**: stocks are grouped by GICS sub-industry, and each stock's exposure is the **weighted relative strength of its peers in the same sub-industry over the trailing six months**. It is a high-turnover, short-horizon factor. In USE4 itself, only stock-level Momentum (6–12 months, skipping the last 21 days) and GICS industry factors (60) were confirmed. No separate industry momentum factor was confirmed there. Academically, Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999) show industry momentum is strong and explains much of stock momentum.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition (MSCI):** the industry momentum factor reflects the tendency of stocks in "winning" industries to keep performing well, and of those in "losing" industries to keep underperforming. Stocks are grouped by **GICS sub-industry**, and the momentum score is "the weighted relative strength of a company's peers within the same sub-industry over the **trailing six months**". MSCI calls it "a relatively high-turnover factor relevant at short investment horizons" — [MSCI blog: Understanding the Industry-Momentum Factor](https://www.msci.com/www/blog-posts/understanding-the-industry/02044436215) [VENDOR]
- **USE4 Momentum (stock level):** differentiates stocks by their performance over the trailing 6–12 months, excluding the last month (21 days) of returns. USE4 uses 60 GICS-based industry factors, and industry factors are dollar-neutral (measured net of the market) — [MSCI USE4 Empirical Notes (2011)](http://index.cslt.org/mediawiki/images/4/47/MSCI-USE4-201109.pdf); [MSCI USE4 fact sheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/242721/Barra_US_Equity_Model_USE4.pdf/d7625289-cade-4e88-96ae-696219af5b67) [VENDOR]
- **Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999, Journal of Finance):** industry momentum strategies (buy past winning industries, sell past losers) remain highly profitable after controlling for size, book-to-market, individual stock momentum and microstructure effects. **Individual stock momentum becomes significantly weaker once industry momentum is controlled for** — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00146); [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Do-Industries-Explain-Momentum); [Wharton PDF](http://www-stat.wharton.upenn.edu/~steele/Courses/956/Resource/Momentum/MoskowitzGrinblatt99.pdf) [INDEP]
- **Replication:** with US large-cap data for 1998–2007, industry momentum strategies earn higher returns than individual stock momentum strategies — [Fraulo & Nguyen replication (Semantic Scholar)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Do-Industries-Explain-Momentum-A-Replication-of-and-Fraulo-Nguyen/d9d22fca5abe3e650d1c4662c337c15ff29502d2) [INDEP]
- **Mechanism:** a weekly-data study (1962–1998) finds the industry momentum effect comes mainly from *own-autocorrelation* in industry returns, not cross-autocorrelation or differences in mean returns — [Journal of Empirical Finance (ScienceDirect): Industry momentum strategies and autocorrelations in stock returns](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927539803000495) [INDEP]
- A practitioner summary says the M&G method sorts industries on 6–12-month past returns, goes long the top industries and short the bottom ones, and holds for 1–12 months — [Blank Capital Research](https://blankcapitalresearch.com/learn/moskowitz-grinblatt-industry-momentum) [secondary]

### Inferences
- MSCI's definition, "peers' RS excluding (or including?) the stock itself, over 6 months", is the most precise institutional formulation and is directly usable: each stock's industry score = the weighted average (probably by cap) of its peers' RS within the same sub-industry.
- The six-month lookback is the point of convergence for IBD (≈6 months), MSCI GEMTR (6 months) and the academic window (6–12 months).

### Gaps
- MSCI's weighting details (cap or sqrt-cap weighting? excluding the stock itself? decay/half-life? a lag?) and its performance figures (return, t-statistic, correlation with stock momentum) were not obtained; msci.com could not be fetched.
- From memory (NOT verified in this session): USE4's RSTR momentum uses roughly 504 days with an exponential half-life of about 126 days and a 21-day lag. Check this against the USE4 Methodology Notes.
- From memory (NOT verified): M&G use 20 industries and a top-3 minus bottom-3 portfolio, with 6-month formation and 6-month holding, and find that **industry momentum is strongest at a 1-month horizon** (in contrast to stock reversal). Check the exact figures in the Wharton PDF.

## 4. Morningstar classification; StockCharts RRG (JdK RS-Ratio / RS-Momentum); Dorsey Wright / Nasdaq DWA sector RS, point-and-figure and sector Bullish Percent

### Takeaway
Morningstar provides a **classification** (3 super sectors / 11 sectors / 55 industry groups / 145 industries since 2019), not a public strength ranking. RRG plots a smoothed, normalised RS-Ratio against its rate of change (RS-Momentum) in four quadrants. The exact formula is proprietary and **no independent backtest was found**. DWA uses point-and-figure charts of price ratios (RS), "DALI" rankings and sector Bullish Percent (30%/70% thresholds). Its performance evidence comes mainly from DWA itself or third parties that cite DWA.

### Cited Findings
- **Morningstar (2019 revision):** 3 Super Sectors, 11 Sectors, 55 Industry Groups, 145 Industries. The 2010 version had 148 industries and 69 groups. Each company is placed in one industry based on its largest source of revenue and income (public filings plus analyst input). The super sectors are Cyclical/Defensive/Sensitive — [Morningstar Global Equity Classification Structure 2019 (PDF)](https://advisor.morningstar.com/Enterprise/VTC/MorningstarGlobalEquityClassStructure2019v3.pdf); [Morningstar AU](https://morningstar.com.au/learn/investing/225130/morningstar-global-equity-classification-structure); [PlanAdviser 2010](https://www.planadviser.com/morningstar-changes-classification-structure-for-global-equities/) [VENDOR]
- **RRG:** the horizontal axis is the JdK RS-Ratio and the vertical axis is the JdK RS-Momentum, crossing at 100. Quadrants: Leading, Weakening, Lagging, Improving. The expected rotation is clockwise — [StockCharts ChartSchool: RRG Charts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-types/relative-rotation-graphs-rrg-charts); [RRG Research: Construction of Relative Rotation Graphs](https://www.relativerotationgraphs.com/blog/resources/construction-of-relative-rotation-graphs) [VENDOR]
- RS-Momentum is the rate of change of the RS-Ratio. The RS-Ratio lags (price improves relative to the benchmark before the point moves right). The exact formula is not standardised; open-source implementations use different normalised EMA variants — [GitHub RRG-Sector-Rotation-India](https://github.com/AdroitAnandAI/RRG-Sector-Rotation-India); [Medium: A Brief Note on RRG](https://medium.com/@nety1025/a-brief-note-on-relative-rotation-g-2ac369f87cb7) [UNOFFICIAL]
- Vendor material concedes that clockwise rotation "may not always come true". No backtest with out-of-sample returns or costs was found — [SSGA/RRG intro PDF](https://img.iex.nl/IEXProfs/Partner/SSGA/State%20Street%20SPDR%20ETF's%20&%20RRG%20-intro%203%20-%20RRG%20indicatoren%20in%20combinatie%20met%20een%20Relative%20Rotation%20Graph.pdf) [VENDOR]
- **DWA peer RS:** Peer RS Reading = (Stock Price / DWA Sector Index) × 100, plotted on a point-and-figure chart. A double-top/double-bottom *signal* indicates the longer-term trend in RS, while the *column* (X/O) indicates near-term performance against peers — [Nasdaq DWA Glossary](https://dorseywright.nasdaq.com/help/glossary); [DWA Point & Figure Basics (PDF)](https://www.nasdaq.com/docs/DWA-Point-Figure-Basics_0.pdf) [VENDOR]
- **DALI (Dynamic Asset Level Investing):** builds an inventory, runs pairwise RS comparisons, and ranks asset classes and sectors by their RS "tally" score. Portfolios overweight the top-ranked ones and underweight the lowest — [DWA Client Presentation 2015 (PDF)](https://oxlive.dorseywright.com/docs/ClientPresentation_March2015.pdf); [PnF University glossary](https://oxlive.dorseywright.com/university/glossary.html) [VENDOR]
- **Sector Bullish Percent:** the percentage of stocks in a universe/sector that are on a point-and-figure buy signal, plotted from 0–100% with reference lines at **30% and 70%**. Xs (rising) = "offense on the field"; Os = more sell signals. The "sector bell curve" combines the BP positions of all sectors — [DWA Point & Figure Basics](https://www.nasdaq.com/docs/DWA-Point-Figure-Basics_0.pdf); [PMI: Using the P&F Methodology to Manage Risk (Keeton)](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.pminstitute.org/resource/resmgr/imported/06_DorseyWright_Keeton.pdf) [VENDOR]
- Rank construction for DWA indexes (pairwise RS-based ranking for index constituents) is described in Nasdaq methodology documents — [Nasdaq DWA index methodology DWAMLVDM](https://indexes.nasdaqomx.com/docs/methodology_DWAMLVDM.pdf) [VENDOR]
- A claimed "3.7% annual outperformance 1987–2023" comes from a third-party article citing DWA research and could not be verified against a DWA publication — [TradeAlgo P&F guide](https://www.tradealgo.com/trading-guides/technical-analysis/point-and-figure-charts-the-classic-method-for-spotting-breakouts-and-price-targets) [UNVERIFIED]

### Inferences
- Morningstar is useful as a mapping layer (the industry hierarchy), not as a strength signal.
- RRG and DWA RS are relative-strength visualisations or tallies. Their strength lies in a two-dimensional interpretation (level + momentum, or signal + column), but neither has published independent evidence comparable to the academic literature on industry momentum.
- Sector Bullish Percent is a **breadth** measure (share of member stocks on buy signals), so it is conceptually close to "% of stocks above MA" (see section 5).

### Gaps
- The exact JdK formula (proprietary, RRG Research BV) and any peer-reviewed RRG backtest were not found.
- DWA's own sector-rotation performance evidence (for example, the DWA Sector 4 index) was not obtained in primary form.

## 5. Weinstein stage analysis for groups, Minervini on group leadership, and breadth within an industry (% above 50/200-day MA)

### Takeaway
Weinstein uses a top-down process: market stage, then sector/industry-group stage (weekly chart, **30-week MA**: Stage 2 = price above a rising MA) plus **Mansfield RS** (ratio to the index normalised to its own 52-week average, zero line). Minervini has a chapter on "Categories, industry groups and catalysts", but no specific quantitative rule was found. Breadth measures (% of stocks above the 200-day MA) are widely used, but **no rigorous backtest of sector-level breadth as a predictor of sector returns was found**.

### Cited Findings
- **Weinstein:** weekly bars and the 30-week MA classify a chart as Stage 1 (basing), 2 (advancing), 3 (topping) or 4 (declining), based on the MA's slope, price's position relative to it, and relative strength against the market — [LuxAlgo: Weinstein Stage Analysis](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/weinstein-stage-analysis/); [Chartmill: Weinstein Stage Analysis indicators](https://www.chartmill.com/documentation/technical-analysis/indicators/92-Weinstein-Stage-Analysis-Indicators) [secondary]
- The method is top-down: market stage, then sector and industry groups, then stocks. RS comparisons are made against sectors, industry groups and other stocks — [Trading Momentum: Complete Guide to Stage Analysis](https://tradingmomentum.substack.com/p/stan-weinsteins-stage-analysis-the) [secondary]
- **Mansfield RS:** the stock/index ratio normalised to its own 52-week average, so it oscillates around zero. Buy candidates should be positive or turning positive — [Stage Analysis: Mansfield RS in TradingView](https://www.stageanalysis.net/blog/489325/how-to-setup-the-mansfield-relative-strength-indicator-in-tradingview) [practitioner]
- Practitioners maintain group RS tables tracking short, medium and long-term RS changes of US industry groups — [stageanalysis.net US industry groups](https://www.stageanalysis.net/blog/category/us-stocks-industry-groups/p39) [practitioner]
- **Minervini:** *Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard* contains the chapter "Categories, industry groups and catalysts" — [Substack Books n Markets summary](https://booksnmarkets.substack.com/p/trade-like-a-stock-market-wizard); [McGraw-Hill product page](https://www.mheducation.com/highered/mhp/product/trade-like-stock-market-wizard-how-achieve-super-performance-stocks-any-market.html) [VENDOR/book]
- **Breadth (% above 200-day MA):** the only quantitative test found is at the individual stock level, not the sector level. Stocks below the 200-day MA had a slightly higher average forward 21-day return (≈1.7% vs ≈1.3%), interpreted as a short-term rebound effect — [arXiv 2603.15848: Algorithmic Trading Strategy Development and Optimisation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15848) [INDEP, but not a sector test]
- Practitioner heuristic: ≥70% of stocks above the 200-day MA = possible over-exuberance — [TradingView MMTH ideas](https://www.tradingview.com/symbols/INDEX-MMTH/ideas/) [heuristic]; descriptive sector breadth commentary: [StockCharts/Arthur Hill, Aug 2025](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/arthurhill-2025-08-stocks-above-200-day-expands-tech-consolidates-big-banks-lead/); [thetrading.tools market breadth](https://www.thetrading.tools/market-breadth) [descriptive]

### Inferences
- A practical "group in Stage 2" definition can be operationalised as: group index (equal- or cap-weighted) above its 30-week MA, the 30-week MA rising, and Mansfield RS of the group against the benchmark > 0.
- The arXiv finding hints that, at the stock level, "above the 200-day MA" does not automatically mean a higher 1-month return. Breadth should therefore be tested empirically before being used as a grade.

### Gaps
- Minervini's quantitative rule on groups (for example, needing several stocks in the same group breaking out) was not found in a primary source.
- No peer-reviewed study of "% of stocks above the 50/200-day MA within an industry" as a predictor of industry returns was found.
- Weinstein's original text (*Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets*) on group ranking was not read directly.

## 6. Sector-rotation ETF strategies (Faber, Antonacci): lookbacks and results

### Takeaway
Faber (Relative Strength Strategies for Investing) ranks the **10 Fama-French/CRSP sectors** monthly by trailing total return (1, 3, 6, 9, 12 months and combinations), buys the top 1–3, and finds the strategy works across all those lookbacks (beating buy-and-hold roughly 70% of the time in secondary summaries). Antonacci's GEM uses a 12-month lookback with an absolute momentum filter. One independent 2026 decomposition finds the absolute momentum filter did most of the work.

### Cited Findings
- **Faber:** each month, rank the 10 Fama-French CRSP sector groups by trailing total return (including dividends) and invest in the top 1, 2 or 3 sectors. Lookbacks tested: 1, 3, 6, 9 and 12 months plus combinations; top-N from 1 to 9 — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Faber's Sector Rotation Trading Strategy](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/trading-strategies-and-models/trading-strategies/fabers-sector-rotation-trading-strategy); [7 Circles: Relative Strength Strategies](https://the7circles.uk/relative-strength-strategies-meb-faber/); [The Robust Trader](https://therobusttrader.com/meb-fabers-sector-rotation-strategy-what-is-it/) [author's paper; secondary summaries]
- The strategy "worked" at all lookback intervals (1, 3, 6, 9, 12 months). ### Inferences
- Across these practitioner systems, the main lookback choices are 3–12 months, with 6 months as the most common middle ground. Rebalancing is monthly (Faber, GEM) or weekly (Zacks).
- The sensitivity to lookback (Quanterlab) and the coarse sector universe (10 sectors in Faber) show that results from sector rotation do not carry over directly to finer-grained industry grades (about 200 groups).
- The absolute momentum/trend filter (Faber's 10-month MA, GEM's T-bill check) appears to contribute much of the risk reduction, separately from relative-strength ranking.
